# Special titles: cross-domain terms, mathematical forms and look-alike techniques

Scope: titles that need a qualifier, titles and labels with mathematical
notation, and same-discipline techniques whose names look alike.

## Cross-domain term disambiguation

Many terms have well-known, substantively different meanings across disciplines: `entropy` → `Information entropy` / `Thermodynamic entropy`; `transformer` → `Transformer architecture` / `Electrical transformer`; `vector` → `Mathematical vector` / `Cloning vector` / `Attack vector` / `Disease vector`; `domain` → `Function domain` / `Protein domain` / `Internet domain`; `bias` → `Statistical bias` / `Cognitive bias` / `Voltage bias`; `kernel` → `Operating system kernel` / `Kernel method` / `Convolution kernel`. The finite corpus in test (c) is the mechanical floor that `find_collisions.py`, `lint_entry.py` and wiki-lint's scanner flag. **It is not exhaustive:** the same rule applies to any term that meets the tests below.

**Apply these tests before writing the title** of any candidate that is a single common word or a short common-word noun phrase:

- **(a) Wikipedia test.** If the term's canonical Wikipedia landing is a disambiguation page ("Tensor (disambiguation)", "Policy (disambiguation)"), or an article about another sense while this sense's article is itself qualified (the bare "Tree of life" article is the mythological motif; the biology sense is "Tree of life (biology)"), the term is cross-domain ambiguous. When the landing cannot be checked, qualify: an ambiguous bare slug causes silent cross-domain collisions later. When the bare article is this sense and neither (b) nor (c) fires, keep the bare title.
- **(b) Drafting test.** Wanting to open the description with "In machine learning, X is …" or "In biology, X is …" is the ambiguity showing. Drop that hedge and qualify the title instead: `Policy` → `Reinforcement learning policy`, `Return` → `Reinforcement learning return`, so the description reads "A reinforcement learning return is …".
- **(c) Common-noun test.** Single common English nouns (`activation`, `agent`, `attention`, `attribute`, `bias`, `cell`, `classification`, `clustering`, `collinearity`, `domain`, `ensemble`, `entropy`, `feature`, `field`, `filter`, `function`, `gradient`, `inertia`, `kernel`, `label`, `model`, `normalization`, `policy`, `predictor`, `regression`, `return`, `sensitivity`, `shrinkage`, `target`, `temperature`, `tensor`, `transformer`, `vector`) are almost always polysemous and are qualified by default. The rare exception is a phrase whose discipline sense has eclipsed all others (`Logistic regression`). A short common-word phrase with an equally familiar other sense belongs to the corpus too (`online learning`, which also names internet-based education; `tree of life`, also a mythological and religious motif).
- **(d) Compound-noun escape hatch.** A compound that already carries a discipline-specific qualifier (`Linear regression`, `Decision tree`, `Random forest`, `Neural network`) usually disambiguates itself. Apply (a)–(c) to the head noun, not the modifier: `regression` alone is flagged; `linear regression` is not.

**Qualify proactively on first creation.** When (a), (b) or (c) fires, the title must be qualified: never claim the bare slug for one sense, even if the source uses the bare term throughout. **Prefer the most natural English noun- or adjective-form compound** (`Thermodynamic entropy`, `Transformer architecture`) over a terse tag like `Transformer (ML)`. The compound adds a qualifier and keeps the candidate word in the title; an alternative name for the same concept, the kind of form you would record as an alias (`Cluster analysis` for `clustering`, `Categorization` for `classification`), goes in `aliases:` and is never promoted to the title to dodge the parenthetical. **A descriptive parenthetical is the form for terms with no graceful compound** (`Feature (machine learning)`, `Filter (convolution kernel)`, `Clustering (machine learning)` with `cluster-analysis` as an alias, `Tree of life (biology)`); it names the sense and is never an abbreviation like `(ML)`. **The bare slug is reserved and unused:** never `entropy.md`, `transformer.md`, `vector.md` or `feature.md`.

**The canonical name is the contextually resolved sense, not the source's literal wording.** A deep-learning paper writing "the entropy of the predictive distribution" produces a candidate named `Information entropy`. Resolution and creation use the qualified slug, and the body link keeps the source's wording as its label: `[[information-entropy|entropy]]` in an ML context, `[[thermodynamic-entropy|entropy]]` in a thermodynamics one. When the word is the compound's modifier (`transformer` for `Transformer architecture`), the entry's opener introduces it in italics ("or simply the *transformer*"), so the label names the entry. **Never add the bare term as an alias to any entry**: it would capture every other discipline's use of the word. An entry titled `Transformer architecture` aliasing `transformer`, or `Filter (convolution kernel)` aliasing `kernel` and `filter`, is that failure. If such an alias already exists, report it as a semantic-invalid removal proposal and preserve it: wiki-lint's ordinary run removes it under the [alias-removal protocol](../../wiki-lint/references/refactors.md#remove-a-semantic-invalid-alias), which rewrites every inbound resolving link. Distinct concepts sharing linguistic roots (`Cross entropy` and `Information entropy`) get their own entries and ordinary wikilinks.

**Keep every claim on the selected sense.** Qualification controls the entry's scope, not just its filename. Do not transfer another sense's origin, definition or properties to the qualified subject because both share the bare word; *Information entropy*, for example, did not originate in thermodynamics. When the historical or mathematical relationship matters, state it precisely and link the other sense. **Wikilink connected senses only where the connection is substantive:** information and thermodynamic entropy share mathematical roots in Boltzmann's statistical mechanics, so an entry discussing that lineage links the other; a mathematical vector and a disease vector share only a word.

## Base-term and mathematical plain forms

**Base term.** When a title carries a parenthetical disambiguator, its base term, the title without the parenthetical (`Feature` for `Feature (machine learning)`), is what the body's bolded opener, the description's grammatical subject, the item-7 and item-16 title-coherence checks and flashcard line 3 use; line 1's leak check searches that base term. The parenthetical disambiguates the filename and the entry's identity and never appears as the running-prose subject. The slug still derives from the full title (`feature-machine-learning`). A compound qualifier such as `Information entropy` is unaffected: the opener bolds the full compound, which equals the title.

**Mathematical titles.** A canonical mathematical name may use inline LaTeX for a load-bearing symbol (`"$k$-nearest neighbors"`, `"$A^{*}$ search"`), never display math or other Markdown in YAML. Double each LaTeX backslash in YAML source, so `"$\\chi^2$ test"` decodes to `"$\chi^2$ test"`. A single backslash starts a YAML escape, which either fails (`\chi`) or decodes to a control character (`\tau` to a tab); the linters report both as unparseable YAML. The body uses the rendered mathematical form; `description:`, flashcard line 3 and every display label use the meaning-preserving plain form. Formatting commands unwrap, Greek symbols take readable names, and scripts keep their operation or index (`$A^{*}$ search` → `A-star search`; `$\ell_1$ norm` → `ell-one norm`; `$L^{-1}$` → `L-inverse`; `$x^{1/2}$` → `x-to-the-one-half`; `$R^{+}$` → `R-plus`; `$\chi^2$ test` or `χ² test` → `chi-squared test`). A chemical charge reads as a charge (`Ca²⁺` → `Ca2-plus`), a formula with two or more element symbols keeps its subscript digits (`H₂O` → `H2O`), and a single-symbol subscript is spoken (`O₂` → `O-two`). Unicode and LaTeX spellings of one title share the same plain form, so answer and label text never diverge. A Greek letter inside an ordinary name (`α helix`, `β-lactamase`) does not make a mathematical title: it stays Unicode in the description, card line 3 and labels.

**Labels and bold.** Display labels are plain text, so a variable that matters typographically goes in the surrounding prose: "the [[k-nearest-neighbors|k-nearest neighbors]] algorithm uses $k$ training examples", not a label containing `$k$`. Markdown `**…**` does not reach math mode, so LaTeX inside a bold span needs math-mode bold: `\boldsymbol{}` for italic letter variables (the default) and `\mathbf{}` for upright operators and constants, as in `**$\boldsymbol{k}$-nearest neighbors**`. This applies wherever bold and math meet, including the opener of a symbol-bearing title, which bolds the math form. When the complete title is a single inline-math span, outer bold marks that first opener occurrence (`**$R^{+}$**`); this is the only place Markdown emphasis may wrap a lone math span.

## Same-surface-form, different-technique

Unlike cross-domain ambiguity, this is two techniques **inside one discipline** whose names read as near-paraphrases: `Weight tying` (a language model reuses its embedding matrix as the output-layer weights) and `Tying weights` (an autoencoder reuses the transposed encoder weights in its decoder). Detect it when two candidates, from different sources or different sections of one source, have titles that differ only by word order, plural/singular or noun/verb form and would land on the same slug; `find_collisions.py` then returns `adjudicate` on probe (c), (e) or (f). Apply the rules below even when only one of the two is written this run.

- **Both entries take qualified titles**, preferably natural compounds (`Embedding–output weight tying` → `embedding-output-weight-tying.md`, `Autoencoder weight tying` → `autoencoder-weight-tying.md`); a descriptive parenthetical works where no compound reads well. The slug derives from the **full** title, parenthetical included, so a long parenthetical buys a long filename; never abbreviate it (`weight-tying-lm.md`), or item 5's check fails and every later probe looks in the wrong place. Never claim a bare ambiguous form for one sense.
- **Qualified titles do the disambiguating.** Add no sentence whose only purpose is to say the entry differs from its namesake. Link the other entry in body prose only where the source substantively relates the two, under [link-worthiness](writing.md#what-earns-a-wikilink), with a label its title or aliases claim, never the bare shared phrase. Once both exist, each lists the other in its Related footer; for a counterpart this run does not write, report the missing reciprocal item under *Notes for the user*.
- **Surface-form aliases are forbidden across the two.** Neither entry lists `tied-weights` or any other form that resolves naturally to either technique; that would re-create the collision (checklist item 18).
- **No placeholder for the counterpart.** When only one of the two is created, qualify the title you write and record the counterpart's reserved qualified title under *Notes for the user*, so a future source creates it at the right slug.
